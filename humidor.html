📖 The Collection the story behind every stick — click a maker

Arturo Fuente Four generations, one hurricane-proof dream

The Fuente story starts in 1912, when Cuban immigrant Arturo Fuente began rolling cigars in West Tampa. The company survived a factory fire, the Cuban embargo (which cut off its tobacco overnight), and repeated relocations — Nicaragua (burned down in the revolution), Honduras, and finally the Dominican Republic in 1980, where Carlos Fuente Sr. and Jr. built it into the most revered name in Dominican cigars. Their motto: "We will never rush the hands of time."

Añejo Reserva #66 🏆5.7 × 52 · Vault

Born from disaster. In 1998, Hurricane Georges destroyed the wrapper crop for Fuente's legendary Opus X. With no Opus wrapper available, Carlos Fuente Jr. took Connecticut Broadleaf maduro leaf and aged it in cognac barrels as a stopgap — and the "temporary" cigar became a legend in its own right. Released only around Father's Day and Christmas, allocated by hand to retailers, hunted by collectors.

Profile: dark fruit, cocoa, spice, sweet cedar over the Opus X internal blend. Do not burn casually.

Hemingway Signature6 × 47 perfecto

In the early 1980s, Carlos Fuente Jr. revived the nearly extinct art of the perfecto — the tapered, hand-shaped figurado that machine production had killed. He named the line for Ernest Hemingway because the shape recalled the cigars of Papa's era. The Hemingway line single-handedly brought the perfecto back to the industry.

Profile: African Cameroon wrapper — cedar, toasted nuts, baking spice, gentle sweetness. The most elegant smoke in the box.

Hemingway Between the Lines4¾ × 47 perfecto

A modern, harder-to-find Hemingway addition — the name is a nod to reading "between the lines," and some releases wear a rare barber-pole-adjacent presentation. Same revived-perfecto lineage in a shorter, punchier package.

Profile: nutty, cedary Cameroon character. 91-rated.

Don Carlos No. 45¼ × 43

The blend Carlos Fuente Sr. made for himself — his personal cigar, eventually shared with the world and named in his honor. After his death in 2016, the line became a living tribute; "The Man's 80th" Don Carlos was Cigar Aficionado's #2 cigar of 2017.

Profile: Cameroon wrapper, aged Dominican filler — black pepper, cedar, cocoa, leather. Balance for days.

Rosado Sungrown Magnum R 565.6 × 56

Launched in 2007 to showcase rosado ("rosy") sun-grown wrapper — leaf grown in full sunlight to develop thicker, redder, more flavorful skin, a callback to the pre-embargo Cuban rosado wrappers Fuente loved.

Profile: cedar, spice, espresso, smooth sweetness. 94-rated.

2021 Unnamed Reservasmall batch

Fuente's mystery line — released without a name because, as the family tells it, some blends are simply too personal to label. Each year's Unnamed Reserva is a small-batch aged release that lets the tobacco speak anonymously.

Profile: polished, age-smoothed Fuente character.

E.P. Carrillo The comeback king's trilogy

Ernesto Perez-Carrillo Jr. is cigar royalty twice over. His father was a Cuban senator and cigar maker who fled Castro; Ernesto took over the family's small Miami factory, El Credito, and in the 1990s created La Gloria Cubana Serie R — one of the cigars that fueled the 90s boom. He sold to General Cigar, worked there a decade, then in 2009 — in his mid-60s, when most men retire — started over from scratch with his children. The "Family Series" trilogy is the proof it worked: three chapters, three #1 or #2 cigars of the year.

La Historia E-III6 × 54

Chapter one (2014) — "The History." A tribute to the Perez-Carrillo family's Cuban past; the vitolas are named for family members (the E-III honors Ernesto III). #2 Cigar of the Year 2014, 95 pts.

Profile: Mexican San Andrés wrapper — cocoa, coffee, pepper, savory sweetness.

Encore Majestic5⅜ × 52

Chapter two (2018) — the "encore" to La Historia's success. A 100% Nicaraguan puro, its fillers aged in tercios — palm-bark bales, an old Cuban technique that ferments tobacco slower and rounder. #1 Cigar of the Year 2018, 96 pts.

Profile: earth, baking spice, cedar, cocoa. The most balanced of the three.

PledgeSojourn Toro & robusto

Chapter three (2020) — the "pledge" of the family's future. It scored 98 points — the highest top score Cigar Aficionado has ever given a Cigar of the Year — completing a trilogy run no other maker has matched. The wrapper is a dark Connecticut Habano grown in the USA. #1 Cigar of the Year 2020.

Profile: black cherry, licorice, spicy oak, black pepper, espresso. Bourbon's best friend.

Flight night: smoke La Historia → Encore → Pledge in one sitting and taste a blender's decade of evolution, wrapper by wrapper.

Montecristo The old name, the new guard

Montecristo was born in Havana in 1935, named after The Count of Monte Cristo — the novel lectors read aloud to rollers on the factory floor. The non-Cuban rights live with Altadis U.S.A., whose Grupo de Maestros — a council of veteran Cuban-trained masters — handles the premium blends.

Espada Oscuro Guard6 × 50

"Espada" is Spanish for sword — each vitola is named for a part of the blade (Guard, Quillon, Ricasso). The Espada line was Montecristo's first-ever Nicaraguan puro (2014), rolled not in their own house but at the Plasencia factory — making the Espadas cousins to the Plasencias resting beside them. The Oscuro followed as the dark version, wearing a rare, well-aged Habano Rosado Oscuro wrapper.

Profile: dark chocolate, espresso, black pepper, roasted earth. Full-bodied. The house workhorse.

1935 Anniversaryflagship

Named for Montecristo's founding year, blended by the Grupo de Maestros as the brand's modern flagship — the Nicaragua version won very high ratings, and the line is positioned as the best non-Cuban Montecristo ever made.

Profile: polished cocoa, cedar, pepper.

Plasencia Five generations of tobacco, 161 harvests

The Plasencias have grown tobacco continuously since 1865, when Eduardo Plasencia began farming in Cuba's Vuelta Abajo. The family fled Castro in the 1960s, lost everything, rebuilt in Nicaragua — then lost it all again in the Sandinista revolution, and rebuilt once more in Honduras. Today they're the largest grower of premium tobacco on earth, supplying countless famous brands, and only began putting their own name on cigars in 2017.

Cosecha 146 La Musica5 × 50 · $8.20/stick

"Cosecha 146" = the family's 146th harvest since 1865, using organically grown tobacco from the 2011–12 crop. Each vitola is named for an element of Honduran culture — La Musica, the music. #19 Cigar of 2020 (93 pts), now discontinued — which is how two fivers got sniped at well under market.

Profile: earth, cedar, cocoa, citrus, caramel sweetness.

Alma del Fuegothe palate-reset

Part of the Alma (soul) trilogy — Fuerte (strength), Fuego (fire), del Campo (the fields). Fuego celebrates the volcanic soil of Ometepe, an island of twin volcanoes in Lake Nicaragua where the family grows some of its most mineral-rich leaf.

Profile: citrus, white pepper, floral, mineral. The brightest thing in the box.

Foundation Cigar Co. The Wise Man of Estelí

Nicholas Melillo spent years as Drew Estate's head of tobacco — the palate behind Liga Privada — before founding Foundation in 2015. His flagship El Güegüense is named for Nicaragua's famous folk play; the title character, "The Wise Man," is a trickster who outwits colonial authorities. The cigar is a love letter to Nicaraguan folklore and soil.

Wise Man Corojo

The original expression — a Nicaraguan puro draped in Corojo '99, the lively, spicy descendant of classic Cuban wrapper seed.

Profile: cedar, black pepper, citrus, floral, leather.

Wise Man Maduro

The 2021 rebrand's dark expression — San Andrés maduro over the same Güegüense core.

Profile: cocoa, coffee, earth, dark sweetness.

The One-Offs singles with stories

My Father Flor de las Antillas Maduro

My Father is the García family — Don "Pepin" García, the most celebrated Cuban roller-turned-blender of his generation, and his son Jaime. Flor de las Antillas ("Flower of the Antilles") honors Cuba itself, and the original won Cigar of the Year 2012 (96 pts). This is the darker San Andrés-wrapped maduro sibling.

Profile: cocoa, espresso, dark fruit, pepper. Famously flawless construction.

Oliva Serie V Melanio

Named for Melanio Oliva, who grew tobacco in Pinar del Río, Cuba in 1886 — the family patriarch. The Serie V Melanio was the cigar that vaulted Oliva from "great value brand" to elite: the Figurado won #1 Cigar of the Year 2014 (96 pts). Ecuadorian Sumatra wrapper over Jalapa ligero.

Profile: espresso, sweet baking spice, cedar, leather. Pound-for-pound value king.

Aging Room Sonata

Aging Room (Boutique Blends) is Rafael Nodal, a Cuban-born former music professional — every line carries a musical name, with vitolas like "Concerto" and "Forte." Nodal's Quattro Nicaragua "Maestro" won Cigar of the Year 2019, putting the brand on the map. The Sonata is made at AJ Fernandez's factory.

Profile: cedar, pepper, cream, baking spice.

Creekside Broadway Discorona 💎

A shop-exclusive collaboration between Creekside Cigar Co. of Cincinnati (PCA Lounge of the Year 2025) and Crowned Heads, in the quirky Discorona format. Shop exclusives like this are pressed in tiny runs and never return — the least-replaceable cigar in the box.

Profile: to be discovered on a worthy occasion.
